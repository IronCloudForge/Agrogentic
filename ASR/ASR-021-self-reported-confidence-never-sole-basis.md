# ASR-021: An agent's self-reported confidence, accuracy, or completion status must never be the sole basis for trusting its output

**Status:** Draft
**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Driver:** [Family: AI] NIST AI RMF (Measure function — independent measurement, not self-assessment, as the basis for trust)

## Requirement statement
Where an agent reports its own confidence level, accuracy, or task-completion status, that self-report must never by itself be sufficient grounds to trust the underlying output. Some form of independent verification — recomputation, an external detection system, or human review — must corroborate the claim before it is relied upon for a governance decision.

## Architectural significance
A self-report is generated by the same system whose reliability is in question, and provides no evidence independent of that system's own behavior — an agent that is wrong is not thereby prevented from also reporting high confidence in being right. Treating self-reported confidence as sufficient collapses the distinction ASR-006 draws between measured accuracy and plausible-looking output, applied here specifically to the agent's own account of its output rather than a human reviewer's impression of it.

## Corpus evidence
A production enterprise agent-security system is built entirely around this principle in practice: rather than relying on any signal the monitored agent itself reports, it operates as a wholly independent detection layer — observing agent behavior externally and evaluating it against its own detectors, threat intelligence, and policy checks (Uber ADR, MLSys 2026 Industry Track paper; arXiv:2605.17380). A separate open-source governance toolkit frames its audit function around the same question from the auditor's side — "can you prove what happened?" — with tamper-evident records designed to be checked independent of the agent's own account (Microsoft Agent Governance Toolkit; github.com/microsoft/agent-governance-toolkit).
